1. Introduction
    

* What is Disaster Recovery?
    
    Disaster Recovery refers to, accordiing to Google Cloud website, an organization’s ability to restore access to and functionality of its IT infrastructure after a disaster caused by natural events or human actions (including mistakes).
    

2. Types of Disasters
    

Disasters include natural disaster such as heavy rain and human-induced disasters (e.g., cyber-attacks, human error)

For more than 3 years as system administrator of banking system, I’ve watched many different kinds of disasters. Of course, there was disaster caused by human error, like misapplied batch change where employee mistakenly applied the office code for test to production. Malfunctioning of network device is also very common case in large legacy system. These disasters caused at least 10 minutes to investigate cause and address to it. And obviously, if service is not available during that time, it's going to be huge problem for company’s credit.

To deal with these service failure, designing architecture with disaster recovery center is definitely neccesary.

3. Disaster Recovery Center
    

Disaster Recovery Center is secondary site in case primary site is not available due to disaster or service failure, to recover and assure continuity of business without downtime. Let’s see how DRC(Disaster Recovery Center) is going to work if disaster causes service failure to Primary Site.

case -study

Our company’s banking system are located in Gyeonnggi- province and

For example, our coumapany has primary site located in Gyeong-gi province in Korea where most of IT employee related to system administrator. And our secondary site, which is our Disaster Recovery Center is also located in Gyeong-gi province, but about 50km away from primary center. It is designed to geographically separate from the threaat. So for maintenencing infra system, traveling for secondary center is quite common, there are some IT engineer though.

Then how about for cloud system?

AWS’ concept for data center redundancy is related to Region and Availability Zone(AZ). Breifly, region refers to physical location for clusturing datacenter, consiting of at least 3 AZ. And AZ refers to more than one seperated data center that provides networking and connection. It could be rather ambiguous definition. But simply, Multi-AZ System can assure availability for datacenter-level failure in a AZ such as fire or power loss and Multi-Region for entire-region failure such as large-scale events including widespread natural disasters or major connectivity disruptions.

GLSB 그림 별 문장 (step taken for recovery)

Then how to failover from primary site to secondary site?

By using GSLB (Global Service Load Balancing) based on DNS, traffic can be switched to Secondary site. Let’s take a look simple architecture of DR system for AWS.

![](https://d2908q01vomqb2.cloudfront.net/5b384ce32d8cdef02bc3a139d4cac0a22bb029e8/2021/12/23/Figure-1.png align="left")

Diagram above depicts the general architecture for multi-region Active-standby deployment. As you can see there is route53 (or third party product for DNS). To failover successfully to healthy datacenter, redirect traffic to DR by configuring DNS is needed. Of course, there is a lot of things to discuss regarding from detail of configuration to minimize impact to more wider topic RPO/RTO (Recovery Point Objective / Recovery Time Objective).

5. Conclusion(자동판독기 오류, 어떤 장애까지 커버하는 DR센터를 구성할꺼냐의 문제, 트레이드오프)
    

In Octover 2025, due to AWS outage, SAOT(Semi-Automated Offiside Technology) in Premier League matches was temporarily suspended. During the disruption, operations fell back to the traditional manual process. The question is why SAOT couldn’t be failovered with DR system. It comes down to deciding the extent of the DR system to be implemented. In this case, the problem was the DNS name for a service endpoint returned an empty record set, so clients couln’t resolve the hostname to a destination and therefore couldn’t establish connection. It was region-wide failure.

![The stadium screen at the London Stadium showing Igor Thiago had a goal disallowed shortly before half-time](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/553/cpsprodpb/5659/live/035dc4b0-ae57-11f0-8ab7-c1aa2c5b0873.jpg align="left")

If there was identical replica for SAOT system, then it might has been possible to failover and to minimize downtime. But they didn’t. Maybe, designing with complete DR system costs a lot or it is too complicated because SAOT has significantly dependent components in same region. This is one of tradeoff when IT service is designed.

There was about 45minutes of downtime for SAOT in Premire League match (Westham vs. Brentford). 45 minutes of failure In banking system? The Financial Supervisory Service and the government would tear that bank apart. So that is why our bank have DR architecture for especially core-related system and can respond within minutes, because for bank, it’s cheaper than dealing with FSS and the government.
